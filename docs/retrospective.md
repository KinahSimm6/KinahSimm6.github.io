# Project Retrospective

## What Went Well

One of my biggest wins during this project was realizing that my varied background is a strength rather than a weakness. Building the website even took me back to my MySpace days, when I used HTML and CSS to customize profiles. Although I had not worked with these tools in a long time, I was able to reconnect with those skills while also learning GitHub, Markdown, and project management concepts. I also learned how to adapt when the recommended tools were not available to me, such as finding another way to work with GitHub while using a Chromebook.

## What Went Wrong

The biggest challenge was managing the project while dealing with unexpected health issues. During this course, I was diagnosed with ADHD and ischiofemoral impingement (IFI), which helped me understand some of the difficulties I experienced with focus, task completion, and managing my workload while also dealing with pain. I am taking three eight-week CMPA courses at the same time, so falling behind made the final stages of the project feel more overwhelming than they needed to be. I was able to complete the individual pieces of the project, but I struggled with bringing everything together and finishing the final submission.

## What I Would Do Differently

In future courses, I plan to treat the entire course like a project from the beginning. Instead of focusing only on individual assignments as they become due, I would create a schedule at the start of the course, break larger projects into smaller tasks, and regularly check my progress. This experience showed me that completing the work and completing the project are not always the same thing. Just like a project has to be planned, worked through, monitored, and eventually completed, I want to approach each course with that same mindset. Ultimately, completing a college course is its own project, and I am the project manager responsible for getting it across the finish line.
